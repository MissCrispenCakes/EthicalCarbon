Current direction

Neighbourhood Environmental Working Memory

A neighbourhood remembers what its residents notice. This project helps make that memory visible, shared and useful for climate action.

What it is

A shared map that keeps growing.

A participatory climate-learning project. Residents and the EthicalCarbon team walk the neighbourhood together, notice what affects the air and the climate where people live, take simple readings with low-cost sensors, and talk about what they see.

Those observations come together on one map: a working memory of the neighbourhood that is revisited through the year, so changes become visible and questions can be followed up.

For communities, not laboratories. Sensors are used for learning and participation, not as regulatory monitors, and what residents know counts as evidence too.

How a map gets made

Walk, notice, talk, map, return, share.

  1. 01

    Walk

    Residents choose everyday routes: to school, to work, to the bus stop, to the park.

  2. 02

    Notice + sense

    A small air sensor takes readings along the way, and walkers note traffic, idling, construction, smells, shade and green space.

  3. 03

    Talk

    Conversations with neighbours, workshops and expert sessions add what a sensor can't: when it is worse, who is affected, what has changed.

  4. 04

    Map

    Routes, readings, streets and buildings, and residents' notes come together on one map.

  5. 05

    Return

    The routes are walked again across the seasons, so the record stays alive and change can be seen.

  6. 06

    Community art

    The memory becomes something to gather around: displays, prints and projections made with residents, so it is shared beyond the data.

06 · Community art (hypothetical)

The memory as a shape that grows.

Each walk adds a ring. Where the air was worse, the line pushes outward and warms in colour, through the same scale as the map; where it was calmer, it settles inward and stays green.

Residents' notes leave small marks on the ring of the month they were made. Over a year, the neighbourhood draws its own portrait, a shape no single walk could make.

As public art it could be printed, projected on a wall, or grown live at a community event as each new walk is added.

Where the idea comes from

From a research scaffold to a neighbourhood's memory.

The method grew out of research on environmental working memory: the idea that a shared environment can hold a record of what happened in it, so people can return to it, recognize what changed, and continue.

In that research, the working memory was a scaffold built in software, in immersive environments that keep traces of what people did in them. Here the same scaffold is turned outward: externalized into the neighbourhood and realized by the memory footprint of its residents, in the routes they walk, what they notice, and what they choose to record together.
